Executive Summary 

Hypercholesterolemia is an insidious disease and the leading factor to the deadliest killer 

in the United States – cardiovascular disease (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 

2023a). Over 85 million American adults suffer from elevated cholesterol, and treatment can 

prove challenging and may require multiple modalities outside just pharmaceutical intervention – 

such as dietary and other lifestyle changes (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2023b; 

Goldman, 2023). Green tea – among the most common teas consumed worldwide – exerts a 

uniquely antihyperlipidemic and cardioprotective effect that suggests its consideration as a 

potentially-viable nonpharmacological option for hypercholesterolemia (Harvard Health 

Publishing, 2012; Kosugi, n.d.). Green tea is rich in catechin phytochemicals that inhibit 

production of low-density lipoprotein (LDL) – considered by most medical professionals as the 

worst cholesterol for cardiovascular health (Cao et al., 2019; Cui et al., 2020). These excellent 

qualities in conjunction with relatively miniscule risks of adverse effects make a strong case for 

implementing the following proposed benchmark project: A 10-week, clinic-based pilot program 

of green tea supplementation for patients newly-diagnosed with hypercholesterolemia. Program 

costs will be minimal, and a synergistic relationship with local health food businesses can be 

formulated – green tea samples for patients in exchange for advertisement opportunities. The 

author will serve as director of the project implementation team, which will consist of the clinic 

providers, educator, nurses, and dietician. After the recruited patients in the project complete 10 

weeks of daily green tea consumption, their lipid panels will be re-drawn and a post-intervention 

questionnaire will be administered to assess patient satisfaction and the prevalence of adverse 

effects of the program. This project is an investment in the future of the community – it will 

safely promote the cardiac health of patients whilst nurturing beneficial ties to local businesses. GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 5

Green Tea Supplementation: An Effective Lifestyle Modification for Cholesterol Management 

Project Introduction and Rationale 

Hypercholesterolemia is a prevalent issue affecting adults in the United States, with an 

estimated 11.3% currently possessing this diagnosis (MacDonald, 2022). According to the 

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, uncontrolled hypercholesterolemia is a major risk 

factor for cardiovascular disease and events – which are the leading causes of death in the United 

States (2023b). The latest United States Preventive Services Taskforce (USPSTF) guidelines on 

hypercholesterolemia management continue to recommend a conservative approach that favors 

nonpharmacological lifestyle changes before initiating cholesterol-lowering drug therapy 

(Christensen, 2022). According to Rosenson & Cannon (2021), standard lifestyle modifications 

of exercise and dietary changes can take 6 to 12 months to take effect with varied results. If 

lifestyle modifications fail to lower harmful cholesterol levels to target ranges tailored to the 

patient’s cardiovascular risk profile, initiation of cholesterol-lowering drugs will become 

necessary (Rosenson & Cannon, 2021). The most commonly-prescribed cholesterol medication 

is HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (often referred to by their suffix “statins”), which despite their 

well-established safety and efficacy profile can nonetheless produce unpleasant side effects in 

about 10% of recipients (Newman et al., 2019). These rare and mostly harmless side effects 

(typically muscle-related) are so unpleasant and widely-known by the general population that 

many patients, especially male, are hesitant about starting statins (Harvard Health Publishing, 

2019). It is therefore important to equip patients with all the means necessary to avoid escalation 

of cardiovascular risks from uncontrolled cholesterol while also avoiding unnecessary 

pharmacological interventions that impose potential adverse effects (Mayo Clinic, 2023). Green 

tea, approximately one-fifth of all tea consumed, has been found to have significant cholesterol-GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 6

lowering effects that translate to lower rates of cardiovascular disease and events (Harvard 

Health Publishing, 2012; Kosugi, n.d.). As such, offering alternative nonpharmacologic therapies 

like green tea consumption can potentially better accommodate patient preferences for promoting 

their wellbeing. To investigate this opportunity of better managing hypercholesterolemia in 

accordance with conservative USPSTF guidelines, the following intervention research question 

will be used to guide this benchmark project: In adults requiring cholesterol-lowering 

management (P), how does daily green tea supplementation (I), compared to no green tea 

supplementation (C), affect lipid panel levels (O) within three months (T)? 

Literature Synthesis 

Upon review of the latest and highest-ranking evidence published on the effects of green 

tea consumption on cholesterol levels, unanimous agreement is established on the significant 

lipid-lowering influence green tea exerts in a variety of demographics compared to abstaining 

from this tea (Adel Mehraban et al., 2021; Bazyar et al., 2020; Hematinezhad Touli et al., 2022; 

Hosseini et al., 2020; Huang et al., 2018; Li et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023; Neyestani & Nikooyeh, 

2022; Xu et al., 2020; Yuan et al., 2018). Specifically, all authors of the meta-analyses 

specifically agreed that daily ingestion of green tea over typically just two months produced 

statistically-significant drops in serum low-density lipoprotein (LDL) and total cholesterol (TC) 

compared to abstaining from green tea (Hosseini et al., 2020; Huang et al., 2018; Li et al., 2022; 

Li et al., 2023; Neyestani & Nikooyeh, 2022; Xu et al., 2020). Some studies have even identified 

that a combination of aerobic exercise and green tea ingestion synergistically lowers harmful 

cholesterol better than each intervention alone (Hematinezhad Touli et al., 2022; Hosseini et al., 

2020). Biochemical studies have identified that epigallocatechin gallate – a prominent catechin 

phytochemical found in green tea – produces many of green tea’s remarkable health benefits GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 7

through blockade of several hepatic metabolic pathways responsible for LDL production (Cao et 

al., 2019; Cui et al., 2020). The risk profile of green tea consumption is minimal, with rare cases 

of hepatic injury from extremely high doses (upwards of 10 cups per day); potentially impaired 

drug efficacy and increased drug toxicity from concurrent statin, nadolol, sildenafil, tacrolimus, 

or warfarin use; and oxalate kidney stones (among people with histories of oxalate stones) listed 

as more significant side effects (Li et al., 2023; Werba at al., 2018; Xu et al., 2020). The 

cardioprotective effects that green tea can secure secondary to lowering cholesterol – compared 

to its relatively insignificant associated risks – gives a strong case for implementing this cost-

effective intervention as a new recommendation for adults seeking to better control their 

cholesterol levels through lifestyle changes. A detailed analysis of the reviewed evidence can be 

found in Appendix A. 

Project Proposal and Stakeholders 

The proposed benchmark project is to implement a 10-week daily green tea 

supplementation education program for eligible newly-diagnosed hypercholesterolemia patients 

at a primary care clinic (to be called the “Green Tea Education for Cholesterol Control” 

[GTECC] Program). Local green tea suppliers, health care providers, clinic administrators, clinic 

dieticians, clinic educators, clinic nurses, the patients, and the patients’ families are all identified 

stakeholders whose support and cooperativity will ensure project success (Shirey, 2012, p. 399). 

Permission will need to be granted from the administrative leaders of the clinic, the clinic 

educator, and the supervising health care provider – the likely gatekeepers for implementing this 

project. Informed consent must be obtained from the patients. The health care providers, nurses, 

and clinic educators are hopeful allies and champions for this change, as they all possess 

educational backgrounds in evidence-based practice. GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 8

The greatest foreseen hurdle to overcome is recruiting the support of more-hesitant health 

care providers and gatekeepers – due to underlying general skepticism towards holistic health 

practices, improper or incomplete presentation of the current evidence, or general disdain for 

practice change. The second-most challenging barrier is incomplete buy-in and adherence by 

patients due to insufficient education by the prescribing health care provider. Both can be 

minimized by thoroughly discussing and educating the evidence and benefits of the change 

project with all team members, patients, and patient families. Specifically, it is essential that 

approval and support is attained from the aforementioned gatekeepers through their inclusion in 

project proposal and planning meetings. The project proposal content must include a thorough 

discussion of the safety, ethical, legal, and scientific considerations of the project. 

Implementation Plan 

A minimal set of resources are needed to enact the GTECC program: a clinic-based team 

to plan, implement, and evaluate the project; already-existing office devices and supplies to 

make patient educational handouts and signage; and samples of green tea bags for patients 

newly-diagnosed with hypercholesterolemia. 

The author intends to serve as the project director and plans to recruit the involvement 

and support of the clinic’s supervising health care provider, nurse practitioners, educator, nurses, 

and dietician to be part of the implementation team. This team will educate newly-diagnosed 

hypercholesterolemia patients on daily green tea ingestion as a supplement to other lifestyle 

changes to control their cholesterol, using both green tea samples and GTECC handouts as 

reinforcement aides (see Appendix B). These clinicians must screen for significant 

contraindications to initiating green tea supplementation, which includes the following: 

Concurrent use of statins, nadolol, sildenafil, tacrolimus, or warfarin; tea allergy; pre-existing GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 9

liver disease; or oxalate kidney stone history (Li et al., 2023; Werba at al., 2018; Xu et al., 2020). 

The providers and nurses will provide weekly verbal or email feedback to the project director 

and clinic educator, who can revise the GTECC handout (or program overall) if consistent 

feedback warrants such. The project director must pursue this open and frequent communication 

with the rest of the team to ensure the project can evolve according to the needs of the patients 

and facility. 

Timeline and Flowchart 

To secure the greatest chance of success, the benchmark GTECC project will follow the 

pilot-phase implementation timeline plan listed below: 

 Week One, Days One-Five: Assess clinic data in absence of green tea intervention. 

Present benchmark project with clinic data and gain approvals from administration. 

 Week One, Days Six-Seven: Plan pilot phase of benchmark project and create printed 

patient educational handouts (with assistance from educator and supervising clinic 

provider). Educate team on planned pilot phase of benchmark project. 

 Weeks Two-11: Implement pilot phase of benchmark project for 10 weeks, starting with 

collecting consents and pre-intervention lipid panels from recruited participants. Collect 

feedback from team on weekly basis. Collect post-intervention data on week 11. 

 Week 12: Analyze data from pilot phase. Re-evaluate benchmark project and make 

needed revisions from team and patient feedback. Implement change permanently. 

A flowchart depicting this timeline can be found in Appendix C. 

Data Collection Methods 

To properly evaluate the effects of this benchmark project, subjective and objective 

descriptive data must be collected during the pilot phase. Participants must be adults (18 years of GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 10 

age or greater) and possess an established diagnosis of mixed hyperlipidemia. These participants 

must furthermore consist of at least 30 males and 30 females. 

Fasting lipid panels (no food ingested for eight or more hours prior) will be collected 

prior to and following the 10-week course of green tea consumption intervention. Following the 

collection of post-intervention lipid panels, a Likert-style post-intervention questionnaire (PIQ) 

will be administered to the patients (see Appendix D). Likert scores can range from zero to five, 

with a higher score indicating a more favorable perception of green tea supplementation in the 

context of newly-diagnosed hypercholesterolemia. 

Evaluation 

The mean difference between pre- and post-intervention lipid values (LDL, TC, TG, 

HDL) and their respective standard deviations and p-values will be analyzed using IBM SPSS 

(version 29) statistical software. The SPSS software is chosen for its strong reputation and 

popularity among statisticians (Masuadi et al., 2021). The SPSS software will also be used to 

formulate a percentage distribution pie chart of the reported side effects obtained from question 

one of the PIQ (including total number of reports of each side effect), as well as to calculate the 

mean values from the Likert results of questions two through four (Appendix D). 

Cost-Benefit Analysis 

Acquiring and utilizing the resources needed for the GTECC program (see 

Implementation Plan) will impose a relatively small ongoing expense – the majority of which 

will likely stem from maintaining a stock of green tea samples and printed patient educational 

handouts. Costs from top commercial retailers for 150-pack individually-wrapped green tea bags 

per month, a single ream of standard-sized copy paper per eight months, and standard-yield 

double-pack of toner cartridges (for a preexisting laser printer) for an estimated six years of GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 11 

project-only usage are approximately $20, $12, and $150 respectively at the time of this 

benchmark paper’s inception. These estimations assume the printed GTECC handout is single-

sided and -paged, the PIQ is single-sided and -paged, a five-day sample-supply of green tea is 

distributed per newly-recruited patient (one bag per day), and 70 patients are initially-recruited at 

the beginning of the program and 30 per month following. Using these approximated costs 

results in a running monthly cost of only $24. The cost of green tea can be potentially allayed by 

acquiring discounts or donations from local green tea suppliers in exchange for free business 

advertisements featured on the printed handouts. This green tea program will benefit the patients 

and clinic alike at minimal investment: better-controlling patients’ hypercholesterolemia, 

improving cardiovascular health outcomes that may increase the clinic’s candidacy for value-

based program incentives (Lewis et al., 2023), boosting patient satisfaction scores of the clinic, 

and formulating a synergistic relationship with local businesses supplying green tea samples. 

Discussion of Implementation Process 

Anticipating the process of how the project will get implemented revolves around two 

important considerations: Patient outcomes and project leadership. The goal of this benchmark 

project is that patients will experience statistically-significant decreases in their LDL or TC 

levels after 10 weeks of daily green tea consumption. Furthermore, reported side effects should 

be minimal (no more than 10 cases of any listed side effect in PIQ question one) and patient 

satisfaction scores should be positive (mean score of four or higher for questions two through 

four of the PIQ in Appendix D). 

The process of project leadership is anticipated to address the following: Project 

implementation challenges, innovation, self-awareness of the director’s leadership style, 

sustainability, and change management. As previously-stated, a significant challenge to the GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 12 

success of this project lies in the fact that conventional medicine is traditionally apprehensive to 

incorporating homeopathic practices (Worrall, 2018). Reaching a consensus amongst several 

health care providers – who all must ponder the legal, scientific, and ethical considerations to 

this subject – is understandably no small feat. These providers are likely to display hesitancy to 

creating a standardized protocol that attaches their name, license, and reputation to something 

seen as controversial in allopathic professional circles. It must therefore be stressed to each 

provider – in carefully-articulated presentations – how rigorous the body of evidence exists that 

supports using green tea as a potent natural therapy for lowering cholesterol. 

Innovative project planning and implementation comes out of necessity. Clinics must 

primarily focus on delivering cost-effective care that achieves revenue to keep the doors open. 

The common proverb ‘necessity is the mother of invention’ rings true here – limitations to 

budgets can serve to inspire creative and even better solutions than if the limitations never 

existed (Martin, n.d.). Implementing a holistic health project like this benchmark requires 

innovative community-networking ideas. If the aforementioned idea (see Cost-Benefit Analysis) 

proves fruitless, another option is for a local gym to fund the project maintenance costs in 

exchange for exclusive referrals to the gym when lifestyle changes are advised by providers to 

their patients. Other solutions can the invented by the implementation team as the need arises. 

Regarding leadership style, the project director is self-described as participative in 

leadership style (Shonk, 2023). Garnering the support and input of all team members before 

implementing a plan is important to the director, who argues that some of the best project ideas 

are not always found in administrative offices but rather in the minds of the ancillary staff – 

whose contributions to the project can easily go unnoticed. GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 13 

To ensure the benchmark GTECC project proves sustainable, the project director will 

need to (1) identify and foster shared vision with one or two co-leaders, (2) secure long-term and 

mutually-beneficial relationships with local businesses that can supply and support the project, 

and (3) maximize simplicity of project implementation. Firstly, the director will need to identify 

a few exceptionally-gifted and -supportive team members that can co-champion the project 

alongside him to ensure implementation longevity. If the director encounters an unanticipated 

absence during a critical phase of project implementation, the project will collapse without the 

presence of co-leaders backing the project’s survival. Secondly, project sustainability also 

depends on keeping ‘symbiotic’ relationships with local businesses that can supply the materials 

needed to keep costs of the ongoing project low. Thirdly, simplicity of implementation is vital 

for change success. If implementing the project demands any perceived hassle or time wastage 

from the staff essential to keeping the project alive, the team will eventually burn-out from the 

change efforts and either make compromising shortcuts or abandon the project altogether. 

When addressing change management of the project, two crucial tools are needed: Team 

communication and stakeholder management. Firstly, humble and open communication between 

the project leader and the rest of the team is essential (Hassell, 2019). The project director must 

take a humble attitude and be open to criticisms and feedback from the implementation team. 

Neglecting to ensure the team perceives their opinions are heard will make stakeholder buy-in 

impossible – which is the end goal of the second tool. Stakeholder management takes this 

foundation of good communication with stakeholders and goes a step further through keeping 

them engaged, appreciated, and informed (if they are supportive) or at least satisfied (if they are 

critical of the change project) (Vogwell, 2003). Maintaining this balance when managing GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 14 

stakeholders – in addition to addressing the other leadership and patient outcome considerations 

above – is vital for securing a successful process of project implementation. 

Recommendations 

The disease burden imposed by hypercholesterolemia is well-established, yet green tea is 

proven to be an effective nonpharmaceutical tool in combating this condition with minimal risk 

of adverse effects to the patient or cost to primary care clinics. Primary care providers are urged 

to consider that the simple act of educating hypercholesterolemia patients to take green tea daily 

offers a safe, potent, cost-effective, and conservative method for preventing the progression of an 

insidious cardiovascular threat. This innovative approach to managing cardiovascular health will 

prove itself to be every clinic provider’s cup of tea. GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 15

Appendix B 

Green Tea Education for Cholesterol Control (GTECC) 

Patient Education Handout 

Directions 

Drink one to two servings of green tea every morning with food, following package 

instructions for preparation. Acceptable variations of this regime include matcha powder or 

green tea extract capsules, but ensure adequate hydration as directed by health care provider. 

Green tea ingestion is most effective at lowering cholesterol when paired with other lifestyle 

changes like aerobic exercise (Hematinezhad Touli et al., 2022; Hosseini et al., 2020). 

The Science 

Multiple studies have shown that daily green tea ingestion significantly-lowers bad 

cholesterol levels – both LDL and total cholesterol – in as quickly as two months (Hosseini et al., 

2020; Huang et al., 2018; Li et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023; Neyestani & Nikooyeh, 2022; Xu et al., 

2020). The chief compound believed to drive these excellent results is a phytochemical called 

epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) that is abundant in green tea and blocks several liver metabolic 

pathways from creating LDL cholesterol (Cao et al., 2019; Cui et al., 2020). 

Contraindications and Warnings 

Do not drink green tea if any of the following apply to you: Taking a statin drug (e.g., 

atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, etc., nadolol, sildenafil, tacrolimus, or warfarin; tea allergy; history of 

liver disease; or history of oxalate kidney stones (Li et al., 2023; Werba at al., 2018; Xu et al., 

2020). Do not ingest more than five servings of green tea per day. Do not drink green tea on an 

empty stomach. The caffeine content in green tea can cause hyperarousal and difficulty sleeping, 

so avoid drinking green tea relatively late in the day (Poulson & Trevino, n.d.). GREEN TEA FOR CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT 32
