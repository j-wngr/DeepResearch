# Does Green Tea interact with any drugs?

Medically reviewed by [Kristianne Hannemann, PharmD](https://www.drugs.com/support/editor/33/kristianne-hannemann-pharmd.html). Last updated on March 3, 2026.

**Official Answer** by Drugs.com 

Yes, [green tea](http://www.drugs.com/mtm/green-tea.html) can interact with several medicines, including some blood pressure drugs, statins, warfarin, and certain newer drugs for menopause symptoms and cancer. Most problems are linked to high intakes of green tea or green tea extract, not an occasional cup.

## What is green tea?

Green tea comes from the leaves of the plant _Camellia sinensis_ and has been used for centuries for health and wellness. People drink it for heart health, weight loss, energy, and its antioxidant effects.

Green tea is sold as:

*   Hot or iced brewed tea
*   Bottled tea drinks
*   Capsules or tablets (green tea extract)
*   Powdered “concentrate” or “fat burner” supplements

Green tea extract and “fat burner” products usually contain much higher doses than a regular cup of tea and are more likely to cause side effects or drug interactions.

## How can green tea affect medicines?

Green tea contains several active ingredients that can change how medicines work in your body.

*   Caffeine can speed you up and may affect drugs that also act on the brain or heart.
*   Catechins (like EGCG) can change how much of a drug is absorbed from your gut by blocking transport “doors” in the intestines. They can also block proteins in the liver that break down certain medications.
*   Vitamin K in dried green tea leaves can affect blood clotting and interfere with some blood thinners.

Most clinical studies show that green tea catechins often lower the amount of certain medicines that reach the bloodstream, while a few drugs may see increased levels. These changes can make a medicine less effective or increase the risk of side effects

## Key medicines that may interact with green tea

Below are some of the better-studied drug interactions. This is not a complete list.

### 1. Blood pressure medicines (including lisinopril and others)

Green tea may reduce how much of some blood pressure medicines your body absorbs.

*   [Lisinopril](https://www.drugs.com/lisinopril.html) (Prinivil, Qbrelis, Zestril): A study found green tea reduced lisinopril reaching the bloodstream by about two‑thirds, which could make it less effective and raise blood pressure.
*   [Nadolol](https://www.drugs.com/mtm/nadolol.html) (a beta blocker): Green tea cut nadolol levels by about 85% in one study, which could lead to higher heart rate or blood pressure.

If you take lisinopril, nadolol, or other blood pressure pills, avoid large daily amounts of green tea or green tea extract unless your provider says it’s okay. If you drink green tea regularly and your blood pressure is harder to control, tell your doctor or pharmacist.

### 2. Statins (cholesterol-lowering medicines)

Green tea can change how some statins are absorbed.

*   [Atorvastatin](https://www.drugs.com/atorvastatin.html) (Lipitor) and [rosuvastatin](https://www.drugs.com/rosuvastatin.html) (Crestor): Green tea and green tea extract have been shown to reduce the amount of these drugs in the blood in some studies, which might make them less effective at lowering cholesterol.
*   Other statins like [simvastatin](https://www.drugs.com/simvastatin.html) (Zocor) may be less affected, but data are limited.

Older reports suggested green tea could sometimes increase statin levels, raising the risk of muscle damage or liver problems, but newer controlled studies mostly show decreased exposure for most statins.

If you take a statin, avoid high-dose green tea extract unless your prescriber reviews it. Let your prescriber know if your cholesterol numbers change after starting or stopping green tea or a green tea supplement.

### 3. Warfarin (a blood thinner)

Green tea leaves contain vitamin K, which helps blood clot. [Warfarin](https://www.drugs.com/warfarin.html) works by blocking vitamin K, so large changes in vitamin K intake can change your blood clotting test (INR).

*   There is a report of a patient on warfarin whose INR dropped (blood became “thicker”) after drinking ½ to 1 gallon of green tea daily.
*   A lower INR can raise the risk of blood clots, stroke, or other serious problems.

If you start or stop drinking green tea regularly, tell the clinic that checks your INR so they can monitor and adjust your dose if needed. Do not change your warfarin dose on your own.

### 4. Veozah (fezolinetant) for hot flashes

[Veozah](https://www.drugs.com/veozah.html) (fezolinetant) is used to treat hot flashes due to menopause. Your body breaks it down mainly through an enzyme called CYP1A2. Green tea can block this enzyme and may raise drug levels.

*   Lab and clinical data suggest that strong CYP1A2 inhibitors can significantly increase levels of fezolinetant.
*   Because green tea and green tea extracts can inhibit CYP1A2, using them with Veozah is not recommended.

### 5. Raloxifene (Evista) and other hormone-related medicines

Green tea catechins may lower how much raloxifene (Evista) your body absorbs.

*   [Raloxifene](https://www.drugs.com/mtm/raloxifene.html) is used for osteoporosis and to reduce breast cancer risk in some women.
*   If green tea reduces its absorption, the medicine may not work as well.

Green tea might also affect other hormone-related drugs, such as tamoxifen, but the evidence in people is limited.

### 6. Certain cancer medicines

Some cancer drugs may be affected by green tea catechins.

*   Bortezomib ([Velcade](https://www.drugs.com/velcade.html)): Lab studies suggest green tea extract may reduce bortezomib activity, possibly making treatment less effective.
*   [5‑fluorouracil](https://www.drugs.com/mtm/fluorouracil-injection.html) (5‑FU): Large amounts of green tea might increase 5‑FU levels and side effects.

Other targeted cancer medicines, such as some tyrosine kinase inhibitors, may also have interactions, but human data are still emerging.

### 7. Allergy, heart, and other medicines affected by absorption

Green tea can block certain transport proteins in the gut (OATPs), which can reduce how much of some drugs your body absorbs. Clinical studies have shown lower drug levels with green tea for:

*   [Fexofenadine](https://www.drugs.com/fexofenadine.html) (an allergy medicine)
*   [Digoxin](https://www.drugs.com/digoxin.html) (heart medicine)
*   [Folic acid](https://www.drugs.com/folic_acid.html)
*   Nadolol, lisinopril, rosuvastatin, atorvastatin: Significant reductions in blood levels as noted above.

### 8. Medicines that also contain caffeine or affect caffeine

Because green tea contains caffeine, it can add to the caffeine from other sources. Some medicines slow the breakdown of caffeine, which can lead to more side effects like jitteriness, headache, trouble sleeping, or a fast heartbeat. Caffeine can also affect the metabolism of certain medications, making them build up in your body.

Examples include:

*   [Cimetidine](https://www.drugs.com/mtm/cimetidine.html) (Tagamet)
*   [Mexiletine](https://www.drugs.com/mtm/mexiletine.html) (Mexitil)
*   [Terbinafine](https://www.drugs.com/mtm/terbinafine.html) (Lamisil)
*   Some antidepressants and antipsychotics (for example, [clozapine](https://www.drugs.com/clozapine.html))

If you are sensitive to caffeine or take medicine that affects caffeine breakdown, limit green tea and green tea energy products. Watch for symptoms like fast heartbeat, nervousness, or insomnia and report them to your doctor.

This is not complete list of all possible drug interactions with Green Tea, and some interactions may not have been reported. For more information, see:

*   **Related**: [Drug Interactions with Green Tea](https://www.drugs.com/drug-interactions/green-tea.html#list) (in more detail)

## Practical safety tips

Use these steps to help lower your risk:

*   Tell your doctor and pharmacist about all teas, herbs, and supplements you use, including green tea drinks, powders, and capsules.
*   If you take high‑risk medicines (for example, warfarin, Veozah, certain cancer drugs, digoxin, statins, nadolol, or lisinopril), ask before starting or stopping green tea or green tea extract.
*   Avoid large daily amounts of green tea (for example, more than 3–4 cups) or any high‑dose green tea extract unless your healthcare provider says it is safe for you.
*   Watch for changes in how you feel after starting or stopping green tea, such as changes in blood pressure, heart rate, bruising or bleeding, or worsening symptoms of the condition being treated, and report them promptly.
*   Never stop or change the dose of a prescription medicine on your own because of green tea.

This is not all the information you need to know about green tea for safe and effective use and does not take the place of your doctor’s directions. Review the full product information and discuss this information and any questions you have with your doctor or other health care provider.
