Can high doses of green tea extract cause liver damage?   Pro·1 step  ·18 sources    

High doses of green tea extract (GTE), especially in supplement form, have been linked to an increased risk of liver damage. **Doses of green tea extract containing 800 mg or more of EGCG per day are associated with significant elevations in liver enzymes and, in some cases, acute liver injury.** The risk is much lower with traditional brewed green tea.

## Evidence from Human Studies

    *   **Randomized Trials:** Large clinical trials show that high-dose GTE (e.g., 843 mg EGCG/day for 12 months) significantly increases liver enzymes (ALT, AST) compared to placebo, with about 5% of users developing moderate or severe liver abnormalities. These changes reversed after stopping GTE and recurred with re-exposure, strongly suggesting a causal link [Yu 2017](https://consensus.app/papers/effect-of-green-tea-supplements-on-liver-enzyme-elevation-yu-samavat/e08d37b5b0855134aefe16e27c6c5ed6/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)[Acosta 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/hepatotoxicity-with-highdose-green-tea-extract-effect-of-acosta-byham-gray/083a92f1847e5e2bb5bb39475d71b5e8/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w).
    *   **Systematic Reviews:** Reviews of human intervention studies and regulatory assessments confirm that liver toxicity is dose-dependent and rare at typical dietary intakes, but risk rises with concentrated supplements. A safe upper limit for EGCG is estimated at 300–338 mg/day for supplements, with liver effects seen at 800 mg/day or higher [Younes 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/scientific-opinion-on-the-safety-of-green-tea-catechins-younes-aggett/d5be47af5765579e910db8218d2172e6/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)[Hu 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/the-safety-of-green-tea-and-green-tea-extract-consumption-in-hu-webster/33d14eabcd47583baf079defb8f6f050/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)[Dekant 2017](https://consensus.app/papers/safety-assessment-of-green-tea-based-beverages-and-dried-dekant-fujii/1cbd586d365457e084fb85cb76c3ef13/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w).
    *   **Case Reports:** Acute liver failure and hepatitis-like injury have been reported in individuals taking GTE supplements, sometimes requiring hospitalization or liver transplantation. Most cases resolve after stopping the supplement [Patel 2013](https://consensus.app/papers/green-tea-extract-a-potential-cause-of-acute-liver-failure-patel-beer/7c82556469375f3a8f7cb3ff53d13aac/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)[Molinari 2006](https://consensus.app/papers/acute-liver-failure-induced-by-green-tea-extracts-case-molinari-watt/1cd19f08128d56a1993c3cdf1fad90c1/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)[Mazzanti 2015](https://consensus.app/papers/hepatotoxicity-of-green-tea-an-update-mazzanti-sotto/0e37361551365c4b9d1c725d1d67c05d/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)+1 MORE.

## Risk Factors and Mechanisms

    *   **Genetic Susceptibility:** Certain genetic profiles (e.g., UGT1A4 A/C genotype) may increase the risk of liver injury from GTE [Acosta 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/hepatotoxicity-with-highdose-green-tea-extract-effect-of-acosta-byham-gray/083a92f1847e5e2bb5bb39475d71b5e8/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w).
    *   **Form and Dosing:** Bolus (single large) doses and taking GTE on an empty stomach increase risk, while brewed tea is generally safe [Hu 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/the-safety-of-green-tea-and-green-tea-extract-consumption-in-hu-webster/33d14eabcd47583baf079defb8f6f050/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)[Oketch-Rabah 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/united-states-pharmacopeia-usp-comprehensive-review-of-oketch-rabah-roe/51a1b60e76ab5beda9b387203db9399c/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)[Dekant 2017](https://consensus.app/papers/safety-assessment-of-green-tea-based-beverages-and-dried-dekant-fujii/1cbd586d365457e084fb85cb76c3ef13/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w).
    *   **Idiosyncratic Reactions:** Some cases appear to be immune-mediated and unpredictable, with higher risk in women and those using multi-ingredient supplements [Cho 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/liver-injury-caused-by-green-tea-extract-in-pd1-mice-an-cho-wang/a8e99afa0b7a583f81863a248cbda266/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)[Mazzanti 2015](https://consensus.app/papers/hepatotoxicity-of-green-tea-an-update-mazzanti-sotto/0e37361551365c4b9d1c725d1d67c05d/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w)[Navarro 2016](https://consensus.app/papers/liver-injury-from-herbal-and-dietary-supplements-navarro-khan/49fac282d57a56779c24a475ccda2499/?search_id=ocl9alAGSeuj7dN0oSw10w).

## Conclusion

High doses of green tea extract supplements can cause liver damage, especially at or above 800 mg EGCG per day. Traditional brewed green tea is generally safe. Individuals considering GTE supplements should use caution, avoid high doses, and consult healthcare providers if they have liver concerns.

## Patterns and Dose–Response

**Human trials and safety reviews**

    *   Large RCTs of high‑dose extract (~843 mg EGCG/day for 12 months) show more liver enzyme elevations vs placebo; about 5% developed moderate/severe abnormalities that resolved after stopping, sometimes recurring on rechallenge, indicating causality [Yu 2017](https://consensus.app/papers/effect-of-green-tea-supplements-on-liver-enzyme-elevation-yu-samavat/e08d37b5b0855134aefe16e27c6c5ed6/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ)[Acosta 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/hepatotoxicity-with-highdose-green-tea-extract-effect-of-acosta-byham-gray/083a92f1847e5e2bb5bb39475d71b5e8/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ).
    *   EFSA concluded that **≥800 mg EGCG/day in supplements** significantly increases transaminases; traditional infusions at typical intakes (≈90–300 mg EGCG/day) are generally safe, with only rare idiosyncratic cases [Younes 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/scientific-opinion-on-the-safety-of-green-tea-catechins-younes-aggett/d5be47af5765579e910db8218d2172e6/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ).
    *   A systematic review of 34 RCTs found liver‑related events rare and mostly mild enzyme rises, but did note a higher (though imprecise) odds of abnormalities vs placebo [Isomura 2016](https://consensus.app/papers/liverrelated-safety-assessment-of-green-tea-extracts-in-isomura-suzuki/318f643fe6ad5d5bb20734bc724f1531/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ).
    *   USP’s safety review links hepatotoxicity cases to **~140–1000 mg EGCG/day**, especially when taken as single bolus doses on an empty stomach, and now mandates cautionary labeling [Oketch-Rabah 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/united-states-pharmacopeia-usp-comprehensive-review-of-oketch-rabah-roe/51a1b60e76ab5beda9b387203db9399c/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ).

## Mechanisms and Susceptible Populations

    *   Animal and in‑vitro work shows high EGCG can cause oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, membrane damage, and necrosis in hepatocytes [Kučera 2015](https://consensus.app/papers/in-vitro-toxicity-of-epigallocatechin-gallate-in-rat-liver-kuera-mezera/7ad0a246bfaf52fb8dec36edefc0795e/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ)[Ramachandran 2016](https://consensus.app/papers/repeated-dose-studies-with-pure-ramachandran-jayavelu/c00a025394e3546c9e60a5641f982d32/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ)[Lambert 2009](https://consensus.app/papers/hepatotoxicity-of-high-oral-dose-lambert-kennett/bc238992a5615711b4d311e82993a1fd/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ).
    *   Clinical DILI cases show **hepatocellular injury**, often severe, strongly associated with HLA‑B*35:01, supporting an idiosyncratic, immune‑mediated component [Hoofnagle 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/hlab3501-and-green-tea-induced-liver-injury-hoofnagle-bonkovsky/8ec9054a047a594abeec4923fed4ec8a/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ).
    *   Dietary restriction/weight‑loss conditions amplify EGCG hepatotoxicity in mice, suggesting extra risk when using products for slimming while dieting [Shi 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/epigallocatechin-gallate-during-dietary-restriction-shi-zhu/bd9727536a295672b31e5c6d656be44b/?search_id=oBWniD0JSpqn-INpjLHKVQ).

## Summary

Most people tolerate green tea and modest‑dose extracts without liver injury, and patients with NAFLD may even see enzyme improvements in some trials. However, high‑dose green tea extract (especially ≥800 mg EGCG/day, fasting, or for weight loss) has a clear, though uncommon, risk of clinically relevant liver toxicity, influenced by genetics and metabolic state.

## Effects of Green Tea Catechins on Liver Biomarkers

    *   Meta‑analysis of 15 RCTs found **no overall effect on ALT, AST, ALP**; in NAFLD patients green tea modestly _reduced_ enzymes, while in healthy subjects it caused a small but significant increase [Mahmoodi 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/effects-of-green-tea-or-green-tea-catechin-on-liver-enzymes-mahmoodi-hosseini/f905cc1643555594b520523daa0c5040/?search_id=uqmvL8BBQled9vcx6yKC1g).
    *   In NAFLD trials, green tea reduced ALT by ~13 U/L and AST by ~11 U/L, supporting hepatoprotective effects in disease states [Mansour-Ghanaei 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/green-tea-as-a-safe-alternative-approach-for-nonalcoholic-mansour-ghanaei-hadi/853127a44c2054a48ee6a5174d28c4b7/?search_id=uqmvL8BBQled9vcx6yKC1g).
    *   A systematic RCT review (34 trials) reported **rare, mostly mild transaminase elevations**, with an imprecise OR ≈2.1 vs placebo and no serious events [Isomura 2016](https://consensus.app/papers/liverrelated-safety-assessment-of-green-tea-extracts-in-isomura-suzuki/318f643fe6ad5d5bb20734bc724f1531/?search_id=uqmvL8BBQled9vcx6yKC1g).
    *   High‑dose extract (≈843 mg EGCG/day for 12 months) increased mean ALT/AST and raised odds of abnormal liver tests 4–7‑fold vs placebo [Yu 2017](https://consensus.app/papers/effect-of-green-tea-supplements-on-liver-enzyme-elevation-yu-samavat/e08d37b5b0855134aefe16e27c6c5ed6/?search_id=uqmvL8BBQled9vcx6yKC1g)[Yu 2016](https://consensus.app/papers/clinical-trial-of-highdose-green-tea-extract-on-yu/d9a82ef36b6e5b519ebf05ef783b743a/?search_id=uqmvL8BBQled9vcx6yKC1g); EFSA concluded **≥800 mg EGCG/day in supplements consistently raises transaminases**[Younes 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/scientific-opinion-on-the-safety-of-green-tea-catechins-younes-aggett/d5be47af5765579e910db8218d2172e6/?search_id=uqmvL8BBQled9vcx6yKC1g).

## Comparison With Other Herbal Supplements

Direct head‑to‑head clinical comparisons are scarce. Insights come from mixed‑product and network data:

    *   DILIN data show **severe hepatocellular injury** from Garcinia cambogia products, often combined with green tea, with deaths and transplants; peak aminotransferases were very high and HLA‑B*35:01 strongly enriched [Vuppalanchi 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/garcinia-cambogia-either-alone-or-in-combination-with-vuppalanchi-bonkovsky/c11225d4268b55c6ad2c897ea96b7b66/?search_id=uqmvL8BBQled9vcx6yKC1g).
    *   Review of GT‑containing multicomponent products notes **more serious outcomes** when green tea is combined with other hepatotoxic herbs, making attribution difficult but highlighting risk of complex blends [Mazzanti 2015](https://consensus.app/papers/hepatotoxicity-of-green-tea-an-update-mazzanti-sotto/0e37361551365c4b9d1c725d1d67c05d/?search_id=uqmvL8BBQled9vcx6yKC1g).
    *   USP safety review ranks green tea among more frequently implicated herbs but emphasizes that **case numbers are low relative to widespread use**, and risk is highly dose‑ and context‑dependent [Oketch-Rabah 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/united-states-pharmacopeia-usp-comprehensive-review-of-oketch-rabah-roe/51a1b60e76ab5beda9b387203db9399c/?search_id=uqmvL8BBQled9vcx6yKC1g).

## Conclusion

Clinical evidence suggests green tea catechins, at dietary or moderate supplemental doses, are at least as safe for liver biomarkers as many herbal products and can be hepatoprotective in NAFLD. However, high-dose green tea extracts show clear, dose‑related ALT/AST elevations and are not unequivocally safer than other hepatotoxic supplements, particularly in weight‑loss formulations or multicomponent products.
